AI Ethics · Evidence, decisions & responsibility

When AI informs a decision, who can justify it?

I study the ethics of AI-assisted decisions: who bears their consequences, what evidence makes them defensible, and where human authority must remain.

Research foundations · 2015–today

An enduring question, a sharper focus.

My doctoral research asked how heterogeneous biomedical evidence could support explainable inference. I carry that concern into AI ethics today: what warrants a decision, what its evaluation leaves unseen, and who can challenge it.

Research pillars

Three questions define my current agenda.

I connect empirical audits of AI behaviour with questions about legitimate decisions. The studies below are directions for collaboration, alongside the existing work linked in each pillar.

01

Justice beyond aggregate performance

Who experiences a different outcome when systems receive the same evaluation score?

I examine how model updates, rankings and selection rules change who or what is chosen. I treat these changes as a reason to investigate their justification and distribution; change alone does not establish unfairness.

Study to develop

Compare equivalent-performing systems at the level of affected individuals, then examine which changes are necessary, avoidable or unjustified.

Existing foundation

RIDI · evaluation and selection-change audits →
02

Accountability through inspectable evidence

What must be recorded so that a person can question an AI-assisted decision?

I connect the representation and provenance of evidence to the reasons offered for a decision. The goal is to make its assumptions, missing knowledge and limits open to scrutiny.

Study to develop

Trace how changes in source data, terminology or evidence alter a recommendation, and evaluate whether the resulting explanation supports meaningful review.

Existing foundation

Doctoral research · D3 · D4 · biomedical inference →
03

Human agency and limits of delegation

Which judgments may an institution delegate to AI, and which responsibilities must people retain?

I study how people can own, defend and revise a judgment when AI produces part of the answer. Oversight must involve the ability and authority to intervene.

Study to develop

Evaluate when people can detect a system’s limits, challenge its advice and revise their decisions under changed conditions.

A current strand · RIDI

What does an unchanged score leave unseen?

RIDI examines the gap between measured performance and the actions or selections of an AI system. I use this work to motivate an ethical question: what evidence would justify the consequences of changing a system?
