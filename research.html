Research

What happens after the score becomes a decision?

I study when apparently equivalent AI systems lead to different decisions — and how evaluation, evidence and governance should change when those decisions affect people, institutions, clinical systems or education.

Research agenda

Three programs, one research logic.

Across AI evaluation, biomedical informatics and GenAI-enabled education, I ask a common question: what important information is lost when complex system behaviour is compressed into a score, category or generated artifact?

01 · FLAGSHIP PROGRAM

AI Evaluation & Decision Systems

Technical performance is not enough when a system selects, ranks, allocates evidence or changes who receives attention.

Research question: When do equal scores hide different system actions?
Current threads: evaluation–action gap, execution provenance, evaluator-robust rankings and selection identity.
Methods: controlled evaluation, reproducibility audits, ranking analysis and consequential decision tests.
RIDI demo →Read the research idea →
02 · PRIMARY PROGRAM

Biomedical Informatics & Drug Safety

Clinical systems can be precise yet still fail when drug identity, evidence coverage or clinical relevance is represented incompletely.

Research question: How does incomplete or misrepresented biomedical knowledge change a clinical decision?
Current threads: drug identity, DDI knowledge completeness, clinical relevance, negative controls and mechanism–severity evidence.
Methods: knowledge integration, clinical adjudication, real-world exposure analysis and interpretable prioritisation.
ANTI-DDI →D3 research foundations →
03 · PRIMARY PROGRAM

GenAI & Computing Education

AI can accelerate curriculum production while quietly erasing context, values, evidence and commitments accumulated across earlier designs.

Research question: How can AI scale curriculum design without erasing context, values and evidence?
Current threads: Calibrated Universalism, Fidelity Debt, IMAM, CIMT and evidence-rich course engineering.
Methods: design research, requirements traceability, artifact audits and culturally responsive computing.

Current research questions

The questions driving the next studies.

These are the problems I am actively trying to make measurable, testable and useful beyond a single application domain.

Q1 · DECISION IDENTITY

Equal performance ≠ equal action

If two systems receive the same aggregate score, did they actually act on the same items, evidence or people?

Q2 · REPRODUCIBILITY

What must be preserved for exact AI replay?

Which hidden execution choices — batching, tensor shape, evaluation rules or state — can change item-level decisions?

Q3 · CLINICAL RELEVANCE

Clinical relevance ≠ statistical association

How should drug-interaction systems distinguish detectable associations from evidence that deserves clinical attention?

Q4 · CURRICULUM FIDELITY

How do we scale without losing accumulated commitments?

When GenAI accelerates redesign, which cultural, pedagogical or evidentiary requirements disappear before anyone decides to remove them?

Responsible AI across the portfolio

Responsibility is a research standard, not a separate topic.

Across the three programs, I use the same accountability test: can we trace the evidence, identify the human decision point, contest the outcome, and assign responsibility for consequences?

Evidence traceabilityMake the evidence behind a model score or recommendation inspectable.
Human oversightDefine where human judgment enters and what authority it retains.
ContestabilityDesign decisions so they can be questioned, reviewed and corrected.
Accountable consequencesEvaluate what the system actually changes, not only what it predicts.

Research initiative

Noor Decision Intelligence Lab (NDI Lab)

NDI Lab is an independent research initiative that organizes my work around a common question: how do knowledge, evaluation and institutional constraints become real decisions? It connects decision intelligence, responsible AI, biomedical knowledge and education without presenting itself as an official university unit.
